Car value isn’t a fixed number — it depends on what the car can realistically do. A vehicle in good working order is worth more on the private market. A car that won’t start is worth whatever it can return through wrecking or scrap. Understanding which category your car sits in is the first step to knowing what offer to expect.

This guide walks through the key factors that affect car value in Brisbane and South East Queensland, and helps you figure out the most practical path for your situation.

Quick answer — the 3 value buckets

Most cars fall into one of three categories when it comes to value:

Bucket 1: Roadworthy and sellable

  • Drives reliably and passes (or could pass) a safety certificate
  • Registered or straightforward to re-register
  • No major accident damage or structural issues
  • Reasonable km for its age

These cars have the most options: private sale, dealer trade-in, or a car buyer service.

Bucket 2: Damaged, unreliable, or high-cost-to-fix

  • Running but with known mechanical issues or recent failures
  • Accident damage or prior insurance write-off
  • Failed safety certificate — expensive to repair
  • Very high km relative to age

These cars are trickier to sell privately. Buyers will discount heavily for risk. A car removal or wrecker may return a better outcome with less effort.

Bucket 3: Non-running, end-of-life, or scrap

  • Won’t start or can’t be driven
  • Major mechanical failure (engine, gearbox, etc.)
  • Flood, fire, or severe accident damage
  • Unregistered and not worth repairing
  • Value comes from scrap car removal in Brisbane — based on metal, parts, and catalytic converter

The main factors that affect car value

Whether you’re selling privately or getting a removal quote, these are the things that move the number:

  • Year, make, and model — some brands and body types hold value better than others
  • Kilometres — total km driven is one of the biggest indicators of wear
  • Service history — a complete log book adds confidence for buyers
  • Tyre, brake, and suspension condition — wear here signals ongoing costs
  • Body damage and rust — visible or structural damage lowers appeal and value
  • Engine and gearbox condition — any known issues must be disclosed and will be discounted
  • Registration status — registered vehicles are easier to sell; unregistered ones require more steps
  • Parts demand — popular makes can return more through wrecking if demand is high
  • Access for towing — if the car can’t be driven, access difficulty affects logistics and cost

How to estimate your car’s value in 10 minutes

You don’t need a professional appraisal to get a rough sense of where your car sits. Here’s a practical approach:

  1. Gather the basics — note the make, model, year, odometer reading, rego status, and any known issues. Have the VIN handy if you want to do a PPSR (Personal Property Securities Register) check for finance encumbrances.
  2. Decide on your sale path — is this a private sale, dealer trade-in, or removal/wrecker? Each path returns a different number. Private sale is usually highest; removal is fastest with the least effort.
  3. Check comparable listings — search Carsales or Facebook Marketplace for the same year, make, model, and similar km. Be honest about your car’s condition relative to what you see.
  4. Adjust down for condition — if your car has damage, high km, or known mechanical issues, similar listings at better condition will be priced higher than your likely return.

2026–2027 market reality — why quotes change week to week

Used car prices in Australia don’t sit still, and that affects what any car is worth at a given moment. A few things driving movement in 2026–2027:

  • Used car supply and demand — listing volumes and buyer activity fluctuate month to month. Industry reporting from bodies like AADA and market platforms regularly tracks these shifts.
  • Fuel and logistics costs — when fuel prices rise, operational costs for towing and pickup services move with them. This can influence removal pricing.
  • Metal markets — steel, aluminium, and copper prices fluctuate based on global demand. This directly affects what scrap vehicles return at the yard.
  • Parts demand — supply chain disruptions and new car availability affect how much used parts are worth, which flows into wrecking prices.
  • Global disruptions — shipping costs, energy prices, and raw material availability can be affected by international events. These flow into local markets over time, sometimes faster than expected.
  • Catalytic converter value — converters contain small amounts of precious metals used for emissions control. Their value is tied to commodity markets and can shift, which is why a car with its converter intact is generally worth more to a scrapper or wrecker.

The practical takeaway: if you’re comparing quotes from a few weeks ago to what you’re being offered today, the market may have shifted. Always get a current quote before deciding.

 

When car removal is the smarter option (Brisbane)

For some cars, the time and effort of a private sale isn’t worth it. If your car fits one or more of the following, removal is usually the more practical path:

  • Won’t start or drive
  • Unregistered and not worth the cost to repair and re-register
  • Accident-damaged or written off
  • Repair costs outweigh what you’d get selling it

QLD paperwork basics

Before you sell or transfer your car in Queensland, it helps to have these ready:

  • Proof of ownership — your name on the rego papers or transfer documents
  • Photo ID
  • Finance disclosure — if there’s money owing on the car, this needs to be resolved before transfer
  • Keys — have them ready; missing keys may affect the quote or process
  • Safety Certificate (roadworthy) — required for private sale of a registered vehicle in QLD; not required when selling unregistered or to a licensed buyer

Frequently asked questions

Does mileage (km) matter more than age for car value?

Both matter, but kilometres are generally a stronger indicator of wear and remaining vehicle life. A 10-year-old car with 80,000 km will typically be worth more than the same model with 250,000 km — all else being equal.

What if my car is unregistered?

An unregistered car can still be sold. If it’s in reasonable condition you may be able to get a safety certificate and sell it privately. If it’s not worth the cost to register and repair, car removal or wrecking is usually the practical route.

What if my car doesn’t start?

A non-running car has limited private sale appeal — most buyers will walk away. Car removal services will still buy it for its scrap and parts value, and free towing is typically included so you don’t need to move it yourself.

How does accident damage affect car value?

Significantly. Structural repairs, insurer write-offs, or even cosmetic damage lowers private sale value because buyers discount for risk. Even where repairs have been done, disclosed accident history reduces what buyers will pay.

Do missing parts reduce the value?

Yes. Missing parts — whether that’s a catalytic converter, battery, wheels, or engine components — reduce both scrap metal value and parts demand. Always let the buyer or removal service know upfront what’s present and what’s not.

Does a catalytic converter affect the value of my car?

Yes, particularly for scrap and wrecking purposes. Catalytic converters contain small amounts of precious metals used in emissions control systems, which gives them their own market value. A car with its converter intact is worth more to a scrapper than one without.

Do you need a roadworthy to sell privately in Queensland?

In Queensland, a Safety Certificate is required to transfer registration when selling a registered vehicle privately. It is not required when selling an unregistered vehicle, or when selling to a licensed dealer or a car removal service.

Why do car quotes change week to week?

Car values — especially for scrap and wrecking — are tied to used car demand, metal market prices, parts availability, and operating costs like fuel and logistics. These all move, which is why current quotes may differ from what you heard a few weeks ago.
