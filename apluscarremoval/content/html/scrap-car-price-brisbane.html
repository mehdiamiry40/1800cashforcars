    The Two Pillars of Scrap Car Valuation

    When we assess your unwanted vehicle, we aren’t just looking at one number; we are calculating two distinct sources of value. Your final cash offer is based on which of these two pillars yields the higher return.

    Pillar 1: Reusable Parts (The Gold Mine)

    This is the most crucial factor for newer, popular, or less damaged vehicles. If your car has functional or in-demand components, its value can skyrocket past its pure scrap weight.

    • Make, Model, and Age: The more popular your model is on Queensland roads (think Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, or late-model Commodores), the higher the demand for its spare parts. A five-year-old car with a blown engine is worth far more for its body, interior, and electronics than a 30-year-old car.

    • Engine & Transmission: A working (or easily repairable) engine or gearbox is a major asset. This component alone can be worth hundreds, or even thousands, more than the entire car’s weight in scrap steel.

    • Catalytic Converter (The Secret Stash): This part contains trace amounts of Platinum, Palladium, and Rhodium—three extremely valuable precious metals. The type and size of your converter significantly influences your final payout, especially for older, larger engines.

    • Intact Body Panels & Glass: If your doors, bonnet, boot lid, headlights, or windshield survived the damage, they have value as used parts for smash repairs.

    Expert Insight: “Don’t strip your car! It might seem like a good idea to remove the wheels or battery, but we generally pay more for a complete vehicle. Missing major components means more work and less potential resale value for us, which lowers your final cash offer.”
