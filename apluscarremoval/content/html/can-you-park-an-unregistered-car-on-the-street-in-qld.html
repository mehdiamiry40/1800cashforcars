Can You Park an Unregistered Car on the Street in QLD?

It starts with a yellow sticker on the windscreen. Or a neighbour mentioning the council. Or a letter in the mail about a vehicle parked outside your house that you forgot about months ago.

An unregistered car sitting on the street in Queensland can move from minor inconvenience to real expense faster than most people expect — fines, towing, impound fees, daily storage costs.

This guide covers the rules for Brisbane and Logan, what councils can actually do, how to report an unregistered vehicle, and what your options are before things escalate.

Quick Answer — Can You Park an Unregistered Car on the Street in QLD?

Generally, no. An unregistered vehicle should not be left on a public road, kerb, nature strip, or public place in Queensland.

The practical distinction that matters:

  • Private driveway, garage, or yard — generally your property, different rules apply
  • Street, kerb, nature strip, footpath, or public land — council territory; an unregistered vehicle here can be reported, marked, and removed

If the car is unregistered, appears abandoned, is damaged, unsightly, or is blocking access, someone is likely to report it eventually. The question is whether you act first or wait for council to.

Private Property vs Street Parking — Why the Difference Matters

Whether your car is on public or private land changes who has authority over it and what rules apply.

  • A car on your own driveway, in your garage, or within your fenced yard sits on private property. Council’s powers to remove it are more limited, though other obligations (rental agreements, fluid leaks, body corporate rules) may still apply.
  • A car parked on the road, on the kerb, or on the nature strip is on public land managed by the council. This is where the offence provisions kick in.
  • Apartment complexes, strata-titled properties, and private car parks have their own rules separate from public roads — body corporate or property management rules may restrict unregistered vehicles regardless of council action.

If the car has moved from your driveway to the street because it stopped running or rego lapsed, it’s worth sorting the situation promptly.

Brisbane Council: Unmanaged, Abandoned and Hazardous Vehicles

Brisbane City Council uses specific terminology for vehicles causing concern on public land. Under their framework, residents can report vehicles described as:

  • Illegally parked — including unregistered vehicles left on public roads
  • Unmanaged vehicles — vehicles that appear abandoned, are in poor condition, or have been in the same spot for an extended period
  • Hazardous vehicles — vehicles that are blocking footpaths, forcing pedestrians onto the road, have sharp or protruding metal, or broken glass

Reports to Brisbane City Council typically require: the location, registration plate or VIN if visible, make, model, colour, and a description of the condition and how long it has been there.

Once a report is lodged, council will assess the vehicle. If it meets the criteria for action, a notice may be placed on the vehicle giving the owner a period to move or claim it.

Source: Brisbane City Council — illegally parked, abandoned and hazardous vehicles reporting page.

Logan Council: Unregistered and Abandoned Vehicles

Logan City Council is more explicit in how it frames the offence. Logan states it is an offence to:

  • Abandon a vehicle on a road or public place
  • Park an unregistered vehicle on a road

If a vehicle is reported and not moved within the required period, Logan Council may arrange for it to be impounded.

Logan example: Logan Council lists a release fee for impounded light vehicles (under 4.5 tonne GVM) along with possible daily storage fees after the first 28 days. These fees can add up quickly. A vehicle worth a few hundred dollars in scrap can end up costing more to release from impound than it’s worth — before any associated fines are added.

Fee amounts are subject to change. Check the Logan City Council website for current rates at the time you’re reading this.

Source: Logan City Council — abandoned and impounded vehicles page.

What Happens If Council Gets Involved?

The process varies slightly between councils, but the general sequence looks like this:

  1. A neighbour or passerby reports the vehicle via the relevant council’s reporting tool or phone line.
  2. Council checks the vehicle’s location and condition, and attempts to identify the registered owner.
  3. A notice or sticker is placed on the vehicle, giving the owner a set period to move or claim it.
  4. If the vehicle is not moved or the owner does not make contact within that period, council arranges for it to be towed.
  5. The vehicle is impounded. Release fees, towing costs, and daily storage charges then apply from that point.
  6. If unclaimed for a sufficient period, the vehicle may be disposed of.

⚠ Timeframes and processes differ between Brisbane, Logan, Moreton Bay, and other councils. Do not assume your council will operate on the same schedule as a neighbour’s experience.

The Financial Trap — Fines, Tow Costs and Impound Fees

This is where the situation can get expensive fast, particularly for a car that isn’t worth much to begin with.

  • Penalties for parking an unregistered vehicle on a road are typically based on Queensland penalty units. Penalty unit values are updated periodically — check the Queensland Government’s current penalty unit value before assuming any figure you’ve heard is still accurate.
  • Towing costs are a separate charge applied when council arranges removal.
  • Impound release fees apply before the vehicle is returned. These are set by council and vary.
  • Daily storage fees may apply after the vehicle has been held for a certain period (Logan, for example, lists fees applying after 28 days of storage).

Practical example: if your car is worth $400 in scrap value and it costs $600 in release fees and towing to retrieve it from impound — before any fine is considered — arranging removal before council gets involved would have saved money and stress.

Dealing with the situation early is almost always cheaper than dealing with it after council has acted.

Can You Move an Unregistered Car Yourself?

If you want to move the car yourself, there are a few things to be clear on first.

Do not drive an unregistered vehicle on a Queensland road without checking your legal options. Driving unregistered is a separate offence and carries its own penalties.

  • TMR unregistered vehicle permits — Transport and Main Roads (TMR) issues permits that allow an unregistered vehicle to be moved on Queensland roads in certain circumstances. Check the TMR website for current eligibility conditions and how to apply.
  • Towing on a trailer — moving an unregistered vehicle on a trailer may be possible in some cases, though the towing vehicle still needs to comply with relevant road rules.
  • If the car is not roadworthy, not running, has flat tyres, or is missing wheels, driving or pushing it on public roads is not appropriate — arrange towing instead.

What If the Car Is on Your Driveway or Yard?

If the vehicle is on your private property and not causing environmental issues or safety hazards, council’s powers to remove it are more limited. But that doesn’t mean there are no obligations.

  • Rental properties — your lease agreement may prohibit unregistered or unroadworthy vehicles on site.
  • Body corporate properties — strata rules often restrict the type and condition of vehicles kept on common property or allocated spaces.
  • Fluid leaks — an unregistered car leaking oil, coolant, or fuel on a driveway can create environmental and liability concerns over time.
  • Neighbour complaints — even on private property, a vehicle that is attracting complaints, becoming an eyesore, or blocking shared access can create friction worth avoiding.

If the car has been sitting for months with no plan, the path of least resistance is usually sorting it sooner rather than waiting for it to become a bigger problem.

What If You Want to Report an Unregistered Vehicle in Brisbane?

If you’re a neighbour or resident dealing with an unregistered or abandoned vehicle on a public road near your property, here’s how to go about it:

  • Confirm the vehicle is on public land — road, kerb, or nature strip — not on someone’s private property.
  • Collect basic details: street location, registration plate or VIN if visible, make, model, colour, and estimated time the vehicle has been there.
  • Report through the relevant council — Brisbane City Council has an online reporting tool; Logan City Council has a similar process. Do not contact a private towing company to report or remove a vehicle from public land.
  • If the vehicle is causing immediate danger — blocking a footpath, forcing pedestrians onto the road, or presenting a safety hazard — flag this clearly in your report.

Council will assess the report and follow their standard process from there.

To report an unregistered vehicle in Brisbane: use the Brisbane City Council website (search ‘unmanaged vehicles’). For Logan: use the Logan City Council website (search ‘abandoned vehicles’).

Before the Council Sticker Turns Into a Tow: Your Options

If the car is on the street and hasn’t been marked yet, you still have time to act. Practical options:

  • Move it onto private property — if you have a driveway or yard and it’s physically possible to move the car, this buys time to figure out the next step
  • Renew rego — if the car is in reasonable condition and the rego lapsed recently, renewing may be straightforward
  • Obtain a TMR unregistered vehicle permit — if you need to move the car on the road to get it somewhere for repair or sale
  • Arrange a tow to a workshop — if the car needs work but isn’t driveable
  • Arrange removal — if the car is damaged, old, non-running, or simply not worth repairing or re-registering, a scrap car removal option or car removal service is usually the simplest path

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I park an unregistered car on the street in Queensland?

Generally, no. An unregistered vehicle left on a public road, kerb, or nature strip can be reported to council, marked with a notice, and potentially towed and impounded if not moved within the required period.

Can I leave an unregistered car on the nature strip?

No. The nature strip is public land managed by the local council. An unregistered vehicle on the nature strip carries the same risks as one parked on the road — it can be reported and removed.

How do I report an unregistered vehicle in Brisbane?

Use Brisbane City Council’s online reporting tool for unmanaged or abandoned vehicles. Provide the location, plate or VIN if visible, description of the vehicle, and how long it has been there. For Logan, use the Logan City Council website.

What happens if council marks my car as abandoned?

A notice will be placed on the vehicle with a period to move or claim it. If the vehicle isn’t moved within that time, council can arrange towing and impound. Release fees, towing costs, and storage charges then apply.

Can council tow an unregistered car?

Yes. If an unregistered vehicle on a public road hasn’t been moved after a notice period, council has the authority to arrange towing and impound the vehicle. The owner is responsible for all associated fees.

Can I move an unregistered car with a TMR permit?

In some circumstances, yes. TMR issues unregistered vehicle permits for specific situations. Check the TMR website for current eligibility conditions and application details.

What if the car is on my driveway?

A car on your private driveway is generally in a different category to one on a public road. However, rental agreements, body corporate rules, or environmental concerns (fluid leaks) may still apply. Sort it out before it becomes a bigger issue.

Can I sell an unregistered car in Brisbane?

Yes. You can sell an unregistered car without re-registering it first. A car removal service will buy it based on its current condition. You’ll need proof of ownership and photo ID.

Do you remove unregistered cars that don’t start?

Yes. We collect non-running unregistered vehicles. Free towing may be included when we purchase the vehicle. Let us know the access situation when you contact us.

Can you remove a car before council tows it?

Yes — and it’s usually the smarter move. Once a vehicle is impounded, release fees and storage charges add up fast. Arranging removal before council acts avoids those costs entirely.
